
# Creating a Game Trailer

Make your trailer feel like a good verbal pitch

## Common Mistakes

- Indie Game Environment Montage (Do not put it at the beginning)
  - Don't sell an ice cream shop by saying "we have flavors and toppings"
  - Wait until at least half way through the trailer for these montages.

- Showing only mid to late game, often these are too much to process.
  - Player needs to learn what the basic parts of the game before seeing the chaos.

- Showing too many aspects of the game
  - By calling out all features, nothing stands out; it doesn't show THE THING of the game.
  - Pick 1 to 3 game pillars and focus on those and dedicate most of the trailer to it.
 
## Structuring a Trailer
- No logos at the start
- No title cards just for the sake of it
- No voice over just for the sake of it
- No empty environment shots easing the player into it
- A trailer doesn't need to "look or feel like a trailer"
- Just be a short video summarizing what the game is

- Start with anchors, the normal, genre defining things
  (like a chinese food place needs fried rice, lo mein and eggrolls)
- Then show hooks or something that is slightly unique.

## Template

[NAME] is a [GENRE] game about [FANTASY].
You [VERBS + USP].

[COMPLICATIONS] force you to [VERBS] in order to [GOAL].

Rushcremental is an active incremental game about surviving and becoming ruler of the wasteland.
You scavenge for scrap and go drag racing.

## Timing

- 2%: Show a small sliver of player controlled gameplay
- 30%: Show the hook and what you do in the game
- 45%: Explore twists and turns on the hook.
- 15%: Do what you want at the end, once you have a solid foundation.
